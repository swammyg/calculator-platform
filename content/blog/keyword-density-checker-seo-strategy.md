# Keyword Density Checker: Optimise Your SEO Strategy Without Keyword Stuffing

Keyword density is the percentage of words in a piece of text that match a chosen term or phrase. It can help editors spot accidental repetition or verify that a page clearly states its topic. It cannot, by itself, make a page rank. Modern SEO requires useful content, clear intent, accurate information and a good page experience.

## What density can tell you

If a key phrase never appears, the page may be unclear about its subject. If it appears in every sentence, the copy can become unnatural and less useful. A checker turns that impression into a number, but context matters: headings, synonyms, definitions and related questions often matter more than a single repeated phrase.

## Is 1–3% ideal?

Treat percentage ranges as rough editing signals, not rules. A short page can show a high percentage with only a few mentions; a long guide can naturally use a term less often while covering the subject comprehensively. Avoid forcing a phrase into places where a reader would not expect it.

## Avoid keyword stuffing

Keyword stuffing is repeating terms for search engines rather than helping readers. It can make copy awkward, reduce trust and conflict with search spam policies. Google’s guidance emphasises helpful content and warns against content created primarily to manipulate rankings; see [Google Search Central’s spam-policy update](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies).

Instead, answer the question directly, cover related subtopics, use natural variants when they improve clarity and cite strong sources. A useful mortgage guide, for example, should explain deposits, interest, SDLT and affordability—not repeat “mortgage calculator” in every paragraph.

## FAQ

### What is the perfect density?

There is no universal perfect percentage. Aim for clear, natural coverage of the topic.

### Can I stuff keywords?

No. Repetition that harms readability or exists only to manipulate search is a poor strategy.

### Does density affect rankings?

It can help you audit clarity, but strong rankings depend on many factors beyond a count.
