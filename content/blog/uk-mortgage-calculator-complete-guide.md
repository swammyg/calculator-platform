# Complete UK Mortgage Calculator Guide: SDLT and Monthly Payment Breakdown

Buying a home is not just about the advertised mortgage payment. A useful UK mortgage calculator combines property price, deposit, interest rate and term with the costs that continue after completion. Use this guide to understand the estimate, plan for SDLT and test whether a purchase leaves enough room in your budget.

## Mortgage basics: price, deposit, LTV and term

The deposit reduces the amount borrowed. Loan-to-value (LTV) is the loan divided by the property value; a larger deposit usually means a lower LTV and may affect the range of available rates. A repayment mortgage normally pays interest and some capital each month. A longer term can lower the monthly payment but usually increases total interest.

Start at the [UK Mortgage Calculator](https://www.kalko.uk/#/uk-mortgage). Enter the purchase price, deposit, rate and term, then include annual council tax and insurance. The calculator shows an estimate of the loan, monthly repayment, interest over the term and recurring housing costs.

## SDLT: the cost buyers often miss

Stamp Duty Land Tax is charged on slices of a qualifying property price, not normally as one rate on the whole price. The rules can differ for first-time buyers, additional properties and non-UK residents. GOV.UK publishes current [residential SDLT rates](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax/residential-property-rates) and its own calculator; check them before exchanging contracts.

For a standard single residential purchase under the rates effective from April 2025, the first £125,000 is at 0%, the next £125,000 at 2%, and the next slice up to £925,000 at 5%. Reliefs and surcharges can change the answer, so treat a website estimate as a planning figure.

## Three planning examples

For a £250,000 home with a £50,000 deposit, the loan is £200,000. Compare 25- and 30-year terms at the same rate to see the payment/interest trade-off. For a £400,000 purchase with an £80,000 deposit, model a modest rate increase as well as the headline rate. For £600,000, include SDLT, legal fees, surveys, removals and a contingency; the deposit alone is not the cash required to buy.

## Hidden and ongoing costs

Budget for council tax, buildings insurance, maintenance, service charges or ground rent where applicable, utilities and furnishing. Lenders also assess affordability using income, commitments and stress testing; a calculator cannot promise borrowing capacity. Compare the monthly total with take-home pay from the [UK Salary Calculator](https://www.kalko.uk/#/uk-salary-tax) and your broader plan in the [Personal Finance Calculator](https://www.kalko.uk/#/uk-personal-finance).

## First-time buyer checklist

- Save a deposit and an emergency fund separately.
- Obtain a decision in principle, then compare the total cost—not only the rate.
- Check SDLT relief eligibility directly with [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax).
- Budget for surveys, conveyancing and moving.
- Keep credit commitments and income evidence organised before applying.

## FAQ

### What is SDLT?

It is a tax that may be due when buying land or property in England and Northern Ireland. Scotland and Wales have different property transaction taxes.

### How much can I borrow?

Only a lender can decide. Income multiples are not a guarantee; affordability, credit history, deposit and commitments all matter.

### Can I use it for a remortgage?

Yes—enter the remaining balance in place of a new loan scenario, but exclude purchase-only costs such as SDLT.
