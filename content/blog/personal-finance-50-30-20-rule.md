# UK Personal Finance 50/30/20 Rule: Budget Like a Pro

Tired of living payday to payday? The 50/30/20 rule is a simple starting point: roughly 50% of after-tax income for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for saving, investing or extra debt repayment. It is a framework, not a test of discipline. A realistic budget starts with what your life actually costs.

## What the 50/30/20 rule means

Needs are essentials such as housing, utilities, food, transport, minimum debt payments and necessary insurance. Wants are flexible choices: eating out, subscriptions, hobbies, holidays and upgrades. The final 20% supports goals—an emergency fund, pension saving, investments or paying down expensive debt faster.

Use monthly take-home pay, not gross salary. Estimate it with the [UK Salary Calculator](https://www.kalko.uk/#/uk-salary-tax), then enter income, essential spending, discretionary spending and a savings goal in the [Personal Finance Calculator](https://www.kalko.uk/#/uk-personal-finance).

## Why a simple budget can work

Categories reduce decision fatigue. Instead of wondering whether every purchase is “allowed”, you decide how much flexible money is available for the month. It also reveals trade-offs: if essentials absorb 65%, the solution may be reducing a fixed cost, increasing income or temporarily setting a different target—not pretending 50% is possible.

## Examples

At £25,000 annual salary, high rent or commuting costs can make the needs share larger. Focus first on a small emergency buffer and costly debt. At £50,000, compare actual net income with the target amounts and automate a transfer on payday. At £75,000, avoid lifestyle inflation by assigning some pay rises to long-term goals. The correct percentages are the ones you can sustain.

## Adapt it to your life

Families may classify childcare as a need. Someone paying off high-interest debt may direct much of the “wants” category to repayment. A high earner can choose a higher savings percentage; a low-income household may need a 70/20/10 pattern for a while. Review monthly, especially after a move, salary change or new commitment.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with gross income rather than net pay.
- Forgetting annual costs such as insurance, gifts and repairs.
- Treating irregular spending as an emergency every month.
- Saving nothing while carrying very expensive consumer debt.
- Giving up because the first version is imperfect.

## FAQ

### What if I do not earn enough for 20% saving?

Start smaller. A regular amount builds the habit; review benefits, debts and fixed costs, and seek free debt help where needed.

### Should I track every penny?

Not necessarily. Track closely long enough to understand patterns, then choose a level of detail you will maintain.

### What about debt?

Minimum repayments belong in needs. Extra repayment can sit in the goals category, particularly for high-cost borrowing.
